The Nixpkgs core team has announced its dissolution. The decision, communicated on the project's official forum, ends ten months of work marked by achievements, but also by deep weariness with the NixOS governance structure.

In a message signed on behalf of the team, members state that dissolution was necessary to preserve mental health. 'Two years ago we came to the conclusion that resigning is necessary for our health,' they say, referring to the period of activity.

The team is proud of what it built: reform of the committer delegation process, integration of 19 new committers, extension of the merge bot to give more power to maintainers, re-establishing contact with GitHub, and securing a sponsored corporate upgrade to Enterprise Cloud. They also helped classify the GHSA-67f2-674w-6g63 vulnerability and track security risks exposed by it, as well as establishing an initial automation/AI policy.

Despite this, the role did not prove to be the 'light role compatible with active technical contribution' they expected. The lack of new members — only one person applied in the last call — made replacement unfeasible. With a Steering Committee (SC) election approaching, the team preferred to be honest about the circumstances.

Criticism of the SC is harsh. According to the team, the institution 'lacks native instinct for delegation as provided in the constitution' and is not cohesive enough to handle individual decisions. This results in unnecessary micro-management, chronic and poor communication, and lack of clarity about when members speak for themselves or for the collegial body.

The team reports that matters were brought with desired outcomes already attached, that the SC took on issues from delegated areas without involving relevant teams, and that responses to concerns were insufficient and delayed. Coordination on topics like GSoC, grant initiatives, and AI policy fell short; progress on moderation and GitHub owner reform was slow.

'It's no secret that our community has many divisions and disagreements,' they acknowledge. Still, the core team's high-trust consensus model produced quality discussions and good results, including approval of the AI policy by people with divergent views.

The environment, however, took its toll. The team understands the general distrust in governance and how it encourages combative zero-sum approaches. But this contributes to burnout when leadership teams try to act in good faith. The result, they say, is that 'it only rewards those who don't care to listen to the community' and reduces the already small group of experienced contributors willing to participate in governance.

The dissolution is not the result of a single incident, but of long-term patterns. The areas under the team's jurisdiction become without direct owner, with the SC as last resort. Members plan to reduce their involvement with Nixpkgs and do not intend to run for the SC.

Despite everything, they maintain belief in the principles that founded the team: light and clear processes for decision-making and dispute resolution are critical to strengthening Nixpkgs. They hope that, in the future, genuine delegation of technical and collaborative leadership will be possible.

The announcement ends with thanks to all who supported the work and regret that they were unable to resolve problems more comprehensively and sustainably. The community now watches how the SC will respond to the void left by the dissolution.

The full discussion is on discourse.nixos.org, where the announcement was published.
