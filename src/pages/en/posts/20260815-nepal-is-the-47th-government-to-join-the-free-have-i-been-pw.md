The Government of Nepal has just joined the list of countries using Have I Been Pwned (HIBP) to protect their official domains. With this, the country becomes the 47th government integrated into the platform’s free service, according to troyhunt.com.

Nepal’s National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) can now monitor Nepalese governmental domains against data present in HIBP. The tool enables identification of exposures in official email addresses and facilitates rapid response when those accounts appear in a new data breach.

This is precisely the purpose of HIBP’s government service: to empower national cybersecurity teams with enhanced threat monitoring and incident response capabilities. Visibility into compromised credentials and breached accounts within governmental domains is essential to reduce risk.

Nepal joins a growing list of governments and national security teams using HIBP to better understand their exposure. The goal is to protect public departments and governmental resources, decreasing the likelihood that leaked credentials will be exploited by attackers before authorities act.

Nepal’s adoption reinforces the importance of open and accessible tools for the cyber defense of developing nations. Unlike expensive, closed solutions, HIBP offers a democratic pathway for resource-constrained countries to gain visibility into their digital risks.

The platform—created by Troy Hunt—is already a global benchmark for data breach notification. By offering this service free of charge to governments, it helps ensure cybersecurity is not the privilege of a few.

For Nepal, this step is significant. The country faces challenges common to many emerging nations—such as growing digital infrastructure and the need to protect data belonging to millions of citizens. Having the NCSC connected to HIBP represents a practical and immediate advancement.

The integration also demonstrates how international cooperation in digital security can be conducted openly and collaboratively. Rather than relying on proprietary solutions, governments can leverage community-driven initiatives that have already proven their value.

Nepal’s move arrives at a time when cyberattacks against public agencies are becoming increasingly frequent worldwide. Having an early-warning system based on real-world breach data is a fundamental defense.

HIBP does not replace other security layers but provides a crucial advantage: knowing what has already been exposed. With this information, IT teams can reset passwords, block accounts, and investigate incidents before greater damage occurs.

The list of governments on the service continues to grow—and Nepal is the most recent example. The expectation is that other countries will follow suit, strengthening a global network protecting against the malicious use of stolen credentials.

The news was announced on the official HIBP website, which celebrated Nepal’s arrival on the platform. The announcement highlights the service’s role in helping nations safeguard their digital assets.

With Nepal, HIBP reaffirms its commitment to democratic and accessible cybersecurity. After all, protecting government data means protecting citizens themselves.
