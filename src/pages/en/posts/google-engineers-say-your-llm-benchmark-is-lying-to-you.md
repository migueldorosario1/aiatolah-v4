Google engineers are warning that large language model benchmarks can mislead the developers who rely on them, according to BigGo Finance.
The engineers say benchmark results often fail to reflect how a model performs in real-world use.
The critique targets the practice of treating a single score as proof of a model's overall capability.
Benchmarks can be gamed or overfit, which undermines their value as neutral measures, the engineers note.
Developers who pick models based on such scores risk deploying systems that underperform on actual tasks.
The warning adds to a broader debate over how the AI industry evaluates and compares models.
